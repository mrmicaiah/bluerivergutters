## Content Sections

### Section 1: Local Problem Hook (200-250 words)
**H2: Why Wood Rot is Common in {{city}}**

North Alabama's climate is tough on exterior wood:

- **High Humidity** — Alabama summers average 70%+ humidity
- **Heavy Rainfall** — 50+ inches per year
- **Temperature Swings** — Freeze-thaw cycles in winter
- **Clogged Gutters** — Water backs up and soaks into wood
- **Poor Ventilation** — Trapped moisture in soffits

**The rot cycle:**
1. Water gets behind fascia board
2. Wood absorbs moisture
3. Humidity prevents drying
4. Fungi and rot take hold
5. Wood softens and deteriorates
6. Gutters sag and pull away
7. More water damage occurs

*Local angle for {{city}}:*
- Older homes in established neighborhoods often have original fascia
- New construction may use lower-quality materials
- Homes near wooded areas have extra shade and moisture

### Section 2: Warning Signs (150-200 words)
**H2: Signs of Rotten Fascia and Soffit**

**Visual signs:**
- Paint peeling or bubbling
- Visible dark spots or discoloration
- Soft spots when pressed
- Crumbling or flaking wood
- Gutters sagging or pulling away
- Gaps between fascia and roof edge

**Other indicators:**
- Birds, bees, or pests entering at roofline
- Musty smell near eaves
- Water stains on interior ceilings
- Difficulty keeping gutters attached

*Note: Often discovered during gutter cleaning or new gutter estimates*

---

### Section 3: What We Repair (150-200 words)
**H2: What We Repair**

**Fascia Boards**
- The vertical board behind your gutters
- Takes direct exposure from overflowing water
- Most common rot location

**Soffit Panels**
- The horizontal underside of roof overhang
- Ventilation holes can allow moisture entry
- Often damaged by backed-up water

**Trim and Rake Boards**
- Decorative trim along roofline
- Exposed ends vulnerable to moisture

**Roof Decking (Limited)**
- If rot extends to plywood
- Small sections only; major roof work referred out

### Section 4: Process (200-250 words)
**H2: Our Repair Process**

1. **Inspection**
   - Assess extent of damage
   - Check for hidden rot behind visible areas
   - Determine if rot reaches structural members

2. **Gutter Removal**
   - Carefully remove affected gutter sections
   - Save hardware for reinstallation when possible

3. **Wood Removal**
   - Cut out all damaged material
   - Extend removal to solid wood
   - Inspect sheathing and rafters

4. **Preparation**
   - Treat exposed wood with preservative
   - Address moisture source if identified
   - Ensure proper ventilation

5. **Replacement**
   - Install new pressure-treated or composite fascia
   - Match existing dimensions and profile
   - Prime and paint to match (or leave for painter)

6. **Gutter Reinstallation**
   - Reattach gutters to solid wood
   - Ensure proper slope and secure fastening

### Section 5: Why Repair First (150-200 words)
**H2: Why Repair Before New Gutters?**

Installing new gutters on rotten wood is a waste of money:

- **Gutters need solid backing** — Hangers pull out of soft wood
- **Rot spreads** — It doesn't stop on its own
- **Hidden damage** — We often find more rot once gutters are removed
- **Warranty issues** — Gutter warranties may be void if installed on damaged wood
- **Safety** — Heavy gutters on weak fascia can fall

*Our policy:*
> We inspect fascia on every gutter estimate. If we find rot, we'll quote the repair separately. We won't install new gutters on compromised wood.

---

### Section 6: Prevention (150-200 words)
**H2: Preventing Future Wood Rot**

- **Keep gutters clean** — Overflows cause most fascia rot
- **Install gutter guards** — Prevent clogs and overflow
- **Maintain paint/stain** — Sealed wood resists moisture
- **Ensure proper ventilation** — Soffit vents must stay clear
- **Trim overhanging branches** — Reduce shade and debris
- **Address leaks immediately** — Small problems become big fast
- **Annual inspection** — Catch rot early when repair is minimal

*Consider upgrades:*
- Composite/PVC fascia (won't rot)
- Aluminum fascia wrap (protects wood underneath)
