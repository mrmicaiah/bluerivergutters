Why Decatur Homes Need Quality Gutters

Living near the Tennessee River has its perks — but water management isn't one of them. Decatur sits in a natural floodplain, and many neighborhoods experience elevated groundwater levels even during normal rainfall. When your water table is already high, the last thing you need is roof runoff pooling around your foundation. That's where quality gutters become your home's first line of defense.

Decatur's housing stock is wonderfully diverse: grand historic homes in Albany and downtown with original copper gutters that need careful restoration, mid-century ranches in established neighborhoods with aging aluminum systems, and newer construction in Southwest Decatur and along the Highway 31 corridor. Each type presents unique challenges, and we've worked on all of them across Morgan County.

The area near Point Mallard and Wheeler Wildlife Refuge is particularly susceptible to drainage issues due to the low-lying terrain. And throughout Decatur, the combination of heavy rainfall, clay soil, and high humidity creates the perfect conditions for gutter problems — overflow, clogs, rust, and the wood rot that follows when water goes where it shouldn't.
