How Much Do Gutters Cost in North Alabama? 2026 Pricing Guide

2026-01-31 11 min read

"How much will new gutters cost?" It's the first question every homeowner asks, and it deserves a straight answer. Unfortunately, the gutter industry hasn't always been great at transparency — vague quotes, hidden fees, and prices that mysteriously change once work begins.

We believe you deserve better. This guide breaks down exactly what gutter services cost in North Alabama in 2026, what factors affect your price, and how to make sure you're getting a fair deal. No games, no sales tactics — just the information you need to make a smart decision.

Gutter Installation Costs by Material

The material you choose is the biggest factor in gutter installation cost. Here's what you'll pay in the Huntsville, Madison, and Decatur areas in 2026:

Vinyl Gutters: $3-6 per linear foot installed

Vinyl is the budget option. It's lightweight, doesn't rust, and comes in several colors. But there's a reason it's cheap: vinyl gutters don't hold up well in North Alabama's climate. Our temperature swings — from freezing winter nights to 95-degree summer days — cause vinyl to expand and contract significantly. Seams pull apart. Sections sag. UV exposure makes the material brittle over time.

Most professional gutter companies (including us) don't install vinyl gutters because the callbacks and failures aren't worth it. If you see vinyl gutters advertised at rock-bottom prices, understand you'll likely be replacing them in 5-10 years. They're a temporary solution at best.

Total project cost for vinyl: $450-$1,200 for a typical home (150-200 linear feet)

Aluminum Seamless Gutters: $6-12 per linear foot installed

Aluminum is the standard for residential gutters in North Alabama — and for good reason. It's durable, rust-resistant, affordable, and available in 20+ colors. Seamless aluminum gutters are fabricated on-site from continuous coil, eliminating the leak-prone seams that plague sectional systems.

The price range depends on several factors:

$6-8 per linear foot: Standard 5-inch K-style gutters, single-story home, easy access, no fascia repair needed. This is the entry point for quality seamless gutters.

$8-10 per linear foot: 5-inch or 6-inch gutters, two-story home, standard complexity. Most North Alabama homes fall in this range.

$10-12 per linear foot: 6-inch gutters, complex rooflines, difficult access, multi-story homes, or situations requiring significant fascia repair before installation.

Total project cost for aluminum seamless: $900-$2,400 for a typical home (150-200 linear feet)

Steel Gutters: $9-15 per linear foot installed

Steel gutters offer superior strength and can handle heavy debris loads better than aluminum. They're available in galvanized steel (prone to eventual rust) or galvalume-coated steel (better corrosion resistance). Steel makes sense for commercial applications or homes with exceptional durability needs.

The downside: steel is heavier, requiring more robust mounting. It costs more to install and will eventually show rust in Alabama's humid climate, even with protective coatings. For most residential applications, aluminum provides better value.

Total project cost for steel: $1,350-$3,000 for a typical home

Copper Gutters: $25-40 per linear foot installed

Copper is the premium option — beautiful, incredibly durable, and expensive. Copper gutters develop a distinctive green patina over time and can last 50+ years with minimal maintenance. They're typically found on high-end homes, historic restorations, or properties where aesthetics justify the investment.

Copper requires specialized installation (soldered joints, proper expansion allowances) and costs significantly more than other materials. But for the right home, the results are stunning.

Total project cost for copper: $3,750-$8,000 for a typical home

What Affects Your Installation Price?

Beyond material choice, several factors determine what you'll actually pay:

Linear footage

Gutter pricing is based on linear feet — the total length of gutters your home needs. A 1,500-square-foot ranch might need 120-150 linear feet. A 3,000-square-foot two-story with complex rooflines might need 250-300 linear feet. More footage means higher total cost, though the per-foot price may decrease slightly on larger jobs.

Number of stories

Two-story and three-story homes cost more per linear foot than single-story ranches. Working at height requires different equipment, takes more time, and involves more risk. Expect to add $1-2 per linear foot for each additional story.

Roof complexity

Simple rooflines with straight runs are faster and cheaper to gutter than complex roofs with multiple valleys, dormers, and direction changes. Each corner, end cap, and downspout connection adds labor and materials. A modern farmhouse with a dozen roofline transitions will cost more than a basic ranch with four corners.

Fascia condition

Gutters mount to fascia boards — the trim boards running along your roof edge. If your fascia is rotted, water-damaged, or pulling away, it needs repair before new gutters can be installed. Fascia repair typically adds $5-15 per linear foot depending on damage extent. Many homeowners don't know they have fascia problems until a gutter contractor points it out.

Access and obstacles

Can we easily position equipment around your home? Are there landscaping obstacles, steep slopes, decks, or other features that complicate access? Jobs requiring hand-carrying materials significant distances or working around obstacles take longer and cost more.

Downspout quantity and routing

Downspouts are included in per-linear-foot pricing, but the number and complexity matter. Homes needing extra downspouts (recommended for proper drainage) or complex downspout routing around obstacles may see higher prices.

Removal and disposal

Removing old gutters and disposing of them properly is typically included in installation quotes, but not always. Make sure you know whether removal and hauling is included or an additional charge.

Gutter Guard Costs

Gutter guards keep debris out of your gutters, dramatically reducing cleaning needs. Prices vary significantly based on guard type:

Basic screen guards: $3-5 per linear foot installed

Simple mesh screens that fit over or into gutters. They block large leaves but allow smaller debris like pine needles and roof grit to pass through. Effective for homes with primarily oak or maple trees; less effective for pine-heavy properties.

Micro-mesh guards: $7-12 per linear foot installed

Fine mesh screens with tiny perforations that block virtually all debris while allowing water through. These are the gold standard for North Alabama, where pine needles are common. More expensive, but genuinely effective.

Solid covers with surface tension: $10-15 per linear foot installed

Solid guards that use surface tension to pull water into the gutter while debris slides off the edge. Effective when properly installed, but can struggle with heavy rainfall (water overshoots) and heavy debris loads.

Total project cost for gutter guards:

For a typical home with 150-200 linear feet of gutters:

Basic screens: $450-$1,000

Micro-mesh: $1,050-$2,400

Solid covers: $1,500-$3,000

Gutter guards can be installed on existing gutters (if they're in good condition) or as part of a new gutter installation. Installing guards with new gutters is more efficient and often provides better results than retrofitting existing systems.

Gutter Cleaning Costs

Professional gutter cleaning in North Alabama typically costs:

Single-story homes: $100-175

Two-story homes: $150-250

Large or complex homes: $200-350+

Pricing depends on home size, gutter linear footage, debris level, and accessibility. Heavily clogged gutters that haven't been cleaned in years take longer than routine maintenance cleanings.

A complete cleaning service should include:

Debris removal from all gutters — by hand, not just blown onto your lawn

Downspout flushing to ensure clear flow

System inspection for loose hangers, leaks, and damage

Cleanup of any debris that falls during the process

Most North Alabama homes need cleaning twice yearly — late fall after leaves drop and late spring after pollen and seed season. Homes with heavy tree coverage may need quarterly service.

Comparing Quotes: What to Look For

When you're getting estimates from multiple contractors, make sure you're comparing apples to apples:

Check the specifications

What material and gauge is quoted? Aluminum comes in different thicknesses — .027" is standard residential weight, .032" is heavier-duty. A cheaper quote might be using lighter-gauge material that won't perform as well.

What size gutters and downspouts? A quote for 5-inch gutters with 2x3" downspouts isn't comparable to 6-inch gutters with 3x4" downspouts. The larger system handles more water and costs more — but performs better in North Alabama's heavy storms.

Understand what's included

Does the quote include old gutter removal and disposal? Fascia inspection and minor repairs? All necessary hangers and hardware? Cleanup after installation? The cheapest quote often excludes things that more complete quotes include.

Ask about warranties

What's covered if something goes wrong? How long is the warranty? Does it cover just materials or also workmanship? A contractor standing behind their work with a solid warranty provides value beyond the installation day.

Verify licensing and insurance

Is the contractor licensed to work in Alabama? Do they carry liability insurance and workers' compensation? If an uninsured worker is injured on your property, you could be liable. Ask for certificate of insurance — legitimate contractors provide it readily.

Red Flags in Gutter Quotes

The gutter industry has its share of fly-by-night operators. Watch out for these warning signs:

Pressure to decide immediately: "This price is only good today" is a sales tactic, not a legitimate business practice. A fair price should be available whenever you're ready to proceed.

Dramatically lower than other quotes: If one quote is 40% below the others, something is wrong. They're using inferior materials, cutting corners, not including necessary work, or not planning to complete the job properly.

No written estimate: Any legitimate contractor provides a written, itemized quote specifying materials, scope of work, and total price. Verbal quotes leave too much room for "misunderstanding" when the bill comes.

Large deposits required upfront: Standard practice is no deposit or a small deposit (10-20%) for special-order materials. Requests for 50% or more upfront are red flags — the contractor may be using your money to finance other jobs or may not be financially stable.

Door-to-door sales with urgency: "We're working in your neighborhood today and noticed your gutters need attention" is a classic high-pressure setup. Legitimate contractors rely on referrals, advertising, and reputation — not door-knocking.

Can't provide references: Any established contractor should be able to provide references from recent customers. Reluctance to do so suggests they don't have satisfied customers to point to.

No physical address or local presence: Storm chasers and traveling crews appear after major weather events, do quick work, and disappear before problems emerge. Choose a contractor with an established local presence who will be around when you need service.

Why the Cheapest Quote Usually Isn't the Best Value

We understand the temptation to go with the lowest price. Budgets are real, and nobody wants to overpay. But with gutters, the cheapest option often costs more in the long run:

Inferior materials fail faster: That $4/foot quote using lightweight aluminum or builder-grade hangers will need replacement in 10 years instead of 20. You'll pay for installation twice.

Improper installation causes problems: Gutters installed with incorrect slope don't drain properly. Water stands, debris accumulates, and the system fails. Gutters installed without adequate hangers sag under snow and ice loads. Cutting corners during installation creates problems down the road.

No warranty means no recourse: When the cheap gutters leak in two years, the contractor who installed them is either out of business or won't return calls. You're paying again to fix what should have been done right initially.

Skipped fascia repair compounds damage: Installing new gutters over rotted fascia is pointless — they won't stay attached properly, and the rot continues spreading. Contractors who "don't notice" fascia problems are either inexperienced or deliberately avoiding necessary work to keep quotes low.

The best value isn't the lowest price or the highest price — it's the right price for quality materials, proper installation, and a contractor who stands behind their work. In North Alabama, that's typically in the $8-10 per linear foot range for standard aluminum seamless gutters, installed by an established local company with a solid reputation.

Getting an Accurate Estimate

The only way to get a truly accurate gutter quote is an on-site evaluation. Online calculators and phone estimates are rough approximations at best — they can't account for your home's specific roofline, fascia condition, access challenges, or other factors that affect the actual price.

A thorough estimate visit should include:

Physical measurement of all roof edges requiring gutters

Fascia inspection for damage requiring repair

Evaluation of downspout placement and drainage routing

Discussion of your goals — standard drainage, maximum protection, gutter guards, etc.

Written quote detailing materials, scope, and total price

Most reputable contractors offer free estimates. The visit typically takes 30-45 minutes and results in a detailed quote you can review on your own time without pressure.

North Alabama-Specific Considerations

Our area has unique factors that affect gutter decisions:

Heavy rainfall: North Alabama receives 55+ inches of rain annually, with intense spring and summer storms. Undersized gutters that work fine elsewhere overflow here. Consider 6-inch gutters and 3x4" downspouts for adequate capacity.

Clay soil: Our red clay doesn't absorb water efficiently. Where water goes matters more here than in areas with better drainage. Proper downspout extensions or underground drains are more critical in North Alabama than in many other regions.

Pine needle debris: Pine trees are everywhere in North Alabama, and pine needles pass through standard gutter screens. If you have pine coverage, factor micro-mesh gutter guards into your planning — or budget for frequent cleaning.

Temperature swings: Our climate cycles from freezing to hot regularly. Materials that can't handle thermal expansion (like vinyl) fail prematurely here. Stick with aluminum or better for gutters that last.

Bottom Line: What You'll Actually Pay

For a typical North Alabama home (1,800-2,500 square feet, 150-200 linear feet of gutters, single or two-story):

Budget option (basic aluminum): $1,000-$1,600

Standard quality (seamless aluminum, proper sizing): $1,400-$2,200

Premium (6-inch gutters, micro-mesh guards): $2,500-$4,500

Add $500-$1,500 if significant fascia repair is needed.

These are real prices for real work, not lowball quotes that balloon once work begins or premium prices that exist only to be "discounted." When you get quotes from reputable local contractors, they should fall within these ranges.
