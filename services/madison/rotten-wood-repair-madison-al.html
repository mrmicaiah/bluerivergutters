The Problem

Why Wood Rot is Common in Madison

North Alabama's climate is brutal on exterior wood. Madison summers regularly hit 70%+ humidity, and with 55+ inches of annual rainfall, your fascia boards face constant moisture exposure. Add in clogged gutters that overflow directly onto the wood, and you've got a recipe for rot.

Here's how it typically happens: water from overflowing gutters (or from ice dams in winter) gets behind the fascia board. The wood absorbs moisture. Madison's humidity prevents it from drying out quickly. Fungi take hold and begin breaking down the wood fibers. The wood softens, darkens, and starts to deteriorate. Eventually, the gutter hangers pull out of the compromised wood, your gutters sag, and even more water pours onto the damaged fascia. It's a vicious cycle.

We see this constantly in Madison — both in established neighborhoods with older homes where original fascia has been exposed to decades of moisture, and in newer subdivisions where builders used lower-quality materials that don't hold up to Alabama's climate. Homes with heavy tree coverage have it worse: the shade keeps wood damp longer, and falling debris clogs gutters more frequently.

The good news? Caught early, rotten wood repair is straightforward and affordable. Caught late, you're looking at structural damage, pest infestations, and major repairs. Early detection is everything.

Spot It Early

Signs of Rotten Fascia and Soffit

Peeling or Bubbling Paint

Paint lifting off the fascia is often the first visible sign. Moisture is pushing up from inside the wood, breaking the paint bond.

Dark Spots or Discoloration

Gray, brown, or black patches on fascia boards indicate moisture damage and likely fungal growth. The wood is already compromised.

Soft Spots When Pressed

If you can push your finger into the wood or it feels spongy, rot has progressed significantly. The structural integrity is compromised.

Gutters Sagging or Pulling Away

When gutter hangers pull out of fascia, the wood has softened. This is often how homeowners first notice rot — the gutters tell the story.

Pests at the Roofline

Birds, bees, wasps, or squirrels entering at your roofline usually means they've found soft wood to penetrate. Rot creates easy entry points.

Gaps Between Fascia and Roof

If you can see daylight between the fascia board and the roof edge, the fascia has warped or pulled away due to moisture damage.

Our Scope

What We Repair

Fascia Boards

The vertical board mounted at the edge of your roof, directly behind your gutters. This is the most common rot location because overflowing gutters dump water directly onto it. We remove damaged sections, treat exposed areas with preservative, and install new wood or composite fascia.

Soffit Panels

The horizontal panels under your roof overhang. Soffit rot often happens when backed-up gutter water runs down the fascia and into the soffit area. We repair or replace damaged soffit sections and ensure proper ventilation is restored.

Trim and Rake Boards

Decorative trim along your roofline, including rake boards on gable ends. These are vulnerable where end grain is exposed. We match existing profiles and dimensions for a seamless repair.

Limited Roof Decking

If rot has spread into the plywood sheathing, we can replace small sections. Major roof decking damage is referred to a roofing contractor, but we'll coordinate the work to ensure your gutters are properly reinstalled.

How We Work

Our Repair Process

1

Inspection

We assess the full extent of damage — not just what's visible. Rot often extends beyond the discolored area. We probe suspicious sections and check for hidden damage behind gutters.

2

Gutter Removal

We carefully remove the gutter sections in front of damaged fascia, saving hardware for reinstallation. If gutters are also damaged, we'll discuss replacement options.

3

Wood Removal

We cut out all damaged material, extending removal until we reach solid, healthy wood. Partial repairs that leave rot behind just fail again.

4

Preparation

Exposed wood is treated with preservative. We identify and address the moisture source — whether it's a gutter issue, flashing problem, or ventilation deficiency.

5

Replacement

We install new fascia — either pressure-treated wood, composite, or PVC depending on your preference and budget. New material is primed and painted to match (or left for your painter).

6

Gutter Reinstallation

Gutters are reattached to solid wood with proper hangers. We verify slope, seal connections, and ensure the system drains correctly to prevent future rot.

Important

Why Repair Before New Gutters?

Installing new gutters on rotten wood is throwing money away. Here's why we always inspect fascia before quoting gutter work:

  • ✓ Gutters need solid backing — Hangers pull right out of soft, rotted wood. Your expensive new gutters will sag within months.
  • ✓ Rot doesn't stop on its own — It spreads. Ignoring it today means worse damage tomorrow.
  • ✓ Hidden damage — We frequently find more extensive rot once gutters are removed. Better to know now.
  • ✓ Warranty protection — Gutter warranties may be voided if installed on damaged wood.
  • ✓ Safety — Heavy, water-filled gutters on weakened fascia can pull away suddenly. That's a hazard.

Our Policy

We inspect fascia on every gutter estimate. If we find rot, we quote the repair separately and explain your options. We won't install new gutters on compromised wood — it's not in your best interest, and we stand behind our work.

Stop It Before It Starts

Preventing Future Wood Rot

Keep Gutters Clean

Overflowing gutters cause most fascia rot. Clean twice yearly at minimum — more often if you have heavy tree coverage.

Install Gutter Guards

Prevent clogs permanently. Gutter guards keep debris out so water flows freely instead of overflowing onto fascia.

Maintain Paint/Stain

Sealed wood resists moisture penetration. Repaint fascia every 5-7 years, or sooner if you see peeling.

Annual Inspection

Catch rot early when repair is minimal. A 15-minute inspection can save thousands in repair costs.

Consider Upgrades

Composite/PVC Fascia: Won't rot, ever. Higher upfront cost but zero future rot repairs. Ideal for Madison's humid climate.

Aluminum Fascia Wrap: Metal covering over existing wood fascia. Protects from moisture and eliminates painting. Good option when current fascia is still solid.
