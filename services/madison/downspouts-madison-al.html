Why Downspouts Matter in Madison

Here's a number that should get your attention: a 1,500 square foot roof collects nearly 1,000 gallons of water from just one inch of rain. Madison gets over 55 inches per year. That's roughly 55,000 gallons of water flowing off your roof annually — and every drop needs to go somewhere.

Your gutters collect that water, but it's your downspouts that actually move it away from your house. When downspouts are undersized, clogged, or discharging too close to your foundation, water pools where you don't want it. And here's the kicker: Madison County's red clay soil doesn't absorb water well. It just sits there, pressing against your foundation, seeping into cracks, working its way into your basement or crawlspace.

Many Madison homes — especially those built during the construction boom of the 2000s — have the minimum number of downspouts the code required, not what the home actually needs. Builders saved a few bucks, and now homeowners are dealing with overflow during every heavy storm. Adding downspouts, upsizing existing ones, or extending them farther from the foundation can solve problems that have plagued a home for years.

Red Flags

Signs of Downspout Problems

Gutter Overflow

Water overflowing from gutters during moderate rain — not just heavy storms — usually means downspouts aren't keeping up with the volume.

Erosion at Discharge

Deep ruts or washed-out areas where downspouts empty. Water is hitting the ground too hard or too close to the house.

Foundation Staining

Water marks or staining on your foundation wall below gutters. Water is running down the side of your house instead of through downspouts.

Basement Moisture

Damp basement walls, musty smell, or actual water intrusion. Often caused by downspouts discharging too close to the foundation.

Loose or Pulling Away

Downspouts separating from the house or gutter. This dumps water right against your foundation — the worst possible place.

Visible Damage

Rust, holes, dents, or crushed sections. Damaged downspouts restrict flow and leak water where it shouldn't go.

Technical Details

Proper Downspout Sizing & Placement

Sizing

Downspout size matters more than most homeowners realize. The right size depends on your roof area and local rainfall intensity.

  • 2" x 3" rectangular: Standard residential size, adequate for most situations
  • 3" x 4" rectangular: High-volume option for larger roofs or heavy rainfall areas like Madison
  • Rule of thumb: 1 square inch of downspout per 100 square feet of roof area

Placement

Where you put downspouts is just as important as how many you have:

  • One downspout per 20-30 linear feet of gutter
  • At low points and corners where water naturally collects
  • Away from walkways and high-traffic areas
  • Discharge at least 4-6 feet from foundation

Move Water Farther

Downspout Extensions & Drainage Solutions

For Madison's clay soil conditions, standard 4-foot discharge often isn't enough. We offer several options to move water farther from your foundation:

Above-Ground Extensions

Simple, affordable, and effective. Above-ground extensions carry water 6-10 feet from the foundation. Options include rigid extensions, flexible tubes, and flip-up designs that move out of the way for lawn mowing.

Splash Blocks

Decorative concrete or plastic blocks that catch downspout water and spread it out over a wider area. Best for situations where you can't run an extension and the ground slopes away from the house.

Underground Drains

The premium solution for Madison's drainage challenges. Buried pipes carry water 15-30 feet from your foundation, discharging at the street, a swale, or a drainage area. Out of sight, maintenance-free, and highly effective.
